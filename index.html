Key Findings & Policy Insights
What the data tells us — and what needs to change
🔥The Rent Squeeze is Real
Average rent has grown 18.3% over 2 years, far outpacing Kenya's wage growth of ~4.5% over the same period. The typical Nairobian now spends 47% of income on rent — nearly double the globally recommended 30% threshold.
Data source: BuyRentKenya, KNBS 2024 wage surveys
📐Supply Is The Core Problem
Nairobi needs approximately 250,000 new housing units annually to match demand. Current construction delivers roughly 50,000 units. The 5x gap is the structural driver of rent escalation — demand far outstrips supply at every price tier.
Gap: ~200,000 units per year
🚇Transit Is The Game-Changer
Neighbourhoods within 10 minutes of the SGR or Thika Superhighway command 40% lower rents than CBD-adjacent areas for equivalent units. Investment in transit unlocks affordability without requiring relocation to distant areas.
SGR commute from Athi River: 18 min to CBD
🌍Satellite Towns Are Underrated
Ruiru, Rongai, and Athi River offer 2-bedroom units at prices matching Nairobi bedsitters — yet infrastructure has improved significantly. Remote work penetration is making these areas viable for a growing segment of workers.
Up to 71% savings vs. Westlands equivalent
📊Information Asymmetry Costs Renters
Analysis found a 35% price premium for identical units in "status" vs equivalent "non-status" neighbourhoods. Renters with data make 30% better decisions. This project itself is the intervention — open data access empowers renters.
Premium charged for "postcode prestige": ~35%
🏗️Affordable Housing Policy Gaps
Kenya's Affordable Housing Programme targets 500,000 units but allocations are skewed toward ownership, not rental. 72% of Nairobi residents are renters. The policy mismatch means public investment is not reaching those most in need.
72% of Nairobians are renters, not owners
